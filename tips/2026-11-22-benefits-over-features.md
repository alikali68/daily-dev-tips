# Sell the Benefit, Not the Feature

Features describe what a product does. Benefits describe what the reader gets. "256 GB of storage" is a feature — "carry your entire photo library everywhere you go" is a benefit, and it's the benefit that makes someone buy.

Run every feature through the "So what?" test: keep asking "so what?" until you reach an outcome the reader actually cares about. Then lead with that benefit and use the feature as proof, not as the headline. One benefit per sentence — stacking three outcomes in a single line dilutes all of them, and a reader who has to work to find the point will simply leave.

**Why it matters:** Readers buy outcomes, not specifications — benefit-led copy converts because it answers the only question that matters: "what changes for me?"
