# Grade Skin Tones, Not Just Colors

A photo can have perfect exposure and still feel wrong — usually the skin is the problem. Instead of pushing global saturation or a trendy preset, isolate the skin: in Lightroom or Photoshop, use the HSL panel to nudge only the orange and red channels, keeping hue shifts small (±5–10) so tones stay believable. Sample skin from the forehead, cheek, and shadow side, and aim for consistency across the frame rather than a single “perfect” value — real skin varies with light. Check your grade on a second screen or phone before exporting; a grade that looks warm on a calibrated monitor often turns orange on a mobile display.

**Why it matters:** Viewers forgive a dull background, but they instantly notice skin that looks plastic, grey, or sunburnt — natural skin tones are what make a retouch feel professional.
