# Design Empty States Like They Matter (Because They Do)

Every product has them — the no-results search, the empty cart, the fresh project dashboard — and most are an afterthought: a grey icon and a shrug. A great empty state does three jobs at once: it explains what happened in plain words (not error jargon), it tells the user why, and it offers one clear primary action to move forward. Keep each part under ten words if you can, and let the illustration and copy carry your brand's voice — this is onboarding in disguise. Bonus: instrument them. An empty state that appears constantly is a feature begging to be redesigned.

**Why it matters:** Empty states are many users' first real interaction with a product — good ones turn confusion into momentum; bad ones make the product feel broken.
