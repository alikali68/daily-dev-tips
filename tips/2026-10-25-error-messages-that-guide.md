# Error Messages That Guide, Not Blame

Most error messages tell users they failed — and stop there. A good error message does three things: it says what happened in plain language, explains why it happened, and gives the exact next step to fix it. "Invalid input" helps no one; "Your password needs at least 8 characters — add two more and try again" turns a dead end into a two-second fix. Keep the tone calm and human, never blame the user, and place the message right next to the field or action that caused it. Test your errors the same way you test your success flows — because this is where trust is won or lost.

**Why it matters:** Users judge a product by how it treats them when things go wrong — a clear error keeps them moving instead of sending them to a competitor.
