# Build Hierarchy with Scale and Weight, Not Just Color

When everything on a page shouts in a different color, nothing stands out — color is the weakest hierarchy tool because it competes with brand palettes, accessibility contrast rules, and the reader's own attention. Scale and weight do the heavy lifting instead: a headline at 3x the body size with a bold weight reads as "most important" before a single color is perceived. Use a clear ratio between levels — for example, 32px bold for the headline, 20px semibold for subheads, 16px regular for body — and keep the steps consistent across the whole design so readers learn the system once and navigate it unconsciously. Whitespace is the third lever: generous space above a headline isolates it and raises its perceived importance without adding a single visual element. Reserve color for one job only — the single action or accent you want the eye to land on last, like a call-to-action button or a key figure.

**Why it matters:** hierarchy built on scale, weight, and space survives grayscale printing, color blindness, and brand-color changes — color-only hierarchy breaks the moment any of those change.
