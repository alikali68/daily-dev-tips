# Write for One Reader, Not an Audience

Most web copy fails because it tries to speak to "everyone" — and an audience of everyone is an audience of no one. Before you write a single word, picture one real person: give them a name, a job, a problem they need solved right now. Then write directly to that person, as if you were answering their question in a conversation. You will naturally drop the jargon, the hedging, and the empty corporate filler, because none of it survives a real conversation. Your sentences get shorter, your verbs get stronger, and your call to action stops sounding like a committee wrote it. The paradox is that copy written for one reader reads as more personal to every reader — because everyone recognizes the feeling of being spoken to directly.

**Why it matters:** Readers don't trust copy that addresses a crowd; they trust copy that feels written for them. One imagined reader is the fastest way to get there.
