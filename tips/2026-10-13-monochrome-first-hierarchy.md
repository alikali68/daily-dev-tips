# Use Size, Weight, and Space for Hierarchy — Save Color for Accent

Many designers reach for color first to separate headline, subhead, and body. Try building hierarchy with type size, font weight, and whitespace alone, keeping the whole composition in one ink color. When the layout already reads clearly in monochrome, any color you add later becomes a deliberate accent instead of a crutch. The squint test catches broken hierarchy fast: squint at your design — if the reading order still survives, your hierarchy is structural. This also keeps your work accessible by default, since nothing depends on color perception alone.

For banners: start the headline at 2.5–3× the body size, keep body at one comfortable weight, and give sections at least as much vertical space as your largest type size. If a heading still doesn't stand out, widen the gap before it before you touch color.

**Why it matters:** hierarchy built on structure survives print, photocopy, and dark-mode inversion — hierarchy built on color doesn't.
