# One Idea Per Paragraph: The Rule That Makes Web Copy Effortless to Read

Online readers do not read — they scan. When a paragraph carries two or three ideas at once, the reader has to work out which one matters, and most will simply move on. Give each paragraph exactly one idea, state it in the first sentence, and use the rest of the paragraph only to support or prove it. If you catch yourself writing "and also" or "another thing to note," that is your signal to start a new paragraph. Short paragraphs also create white space, which makes long pages feel lighter and keeps the reader moving from one idea to the next without friction.

**Why it matters:** One idea per paragraph turns dense copy into a clear, scannable path — readers understand faster, remember more, and stay long enough to reach your call to action.
