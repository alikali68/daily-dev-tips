# The 60-30-10 Color Rule: A Simple Formula for Balanced Palettes

Most unbalanced designs don't suffer from bad colors — they suffer from bad proportions. When every color fights for attention at equal strength, the eye has nowhere to rest and the hierarchy collapses. The classic 60-30-10 rule fixes this: 60% of the design uses one dominant color (backgrounds and large surfaces), 30% uses a secondary color (cards, sections, supporting elements), and just 10% is reserved for an accent color (buttons, links, key highlights). The accent is the only color allowed to shout — if two colors compete for attention, neither wins. A fast way to check your balance: screenshot the design and blur it heavily. The dominant color should own the frame, the secondary should shape the structure, and the accent should appear only in small, deliberate spots.

```css
:root {
  /* 60% — dominant */
  --color-surface: #f4f1ea;
  /* 30% — secondary */
  --color-secondary: #2e4a3d;
  /* 10% — accent */
  --color-accent: #e8590c;
}
```

**Why it matters:** Proportion, not palette size, is what makes a design feel calm and professional — one restrained accent beats five competing colors every time.
