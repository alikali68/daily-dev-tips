# Dodge & Burn: Sculpt Light to Add Dimension to Flat Portraits

Skin retouching often leaves faces looking flat — heavy smoothing removes the natural light falloff that gives a face depth. The pro fix is dodge & burn: paint light and shadow back with intention. Create two Curves layers (one lifted, one pulled down), invert both masks, then use a soft white brush at 3–5% flow to gradually brighten highlights and deepen shadows.

Work on a 50% gray layer set to Soft Light if you prefer — same idea, one layer. Focus on large tonal transitions (cheekbones, jawline, forehead) before ever touching blemishes, and zoom out often: D&B at 100% is where you polish, but the shape is judged at full view. Never dodge skin into clipping — blown highlights can't be recovered and look fake instantly.

**Why it matters:** Mastered D&B does more for a portrait's premium feel than any filter — it's how editorial retouchers get that clean, three-dimensional skin without the plastic look.
