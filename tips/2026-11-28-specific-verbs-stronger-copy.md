# Use Specific Verbs: Weak Verbs Make Weak Copy

"Make", "do", "get", "have", and "is" are the junk food of writing — they fill space and say almost nothing. Swap them for precise verbs and your copy instantly sounds confident and professional: not "we make your workflow better" but "we cut your workflow from five steps to two".

Specific verbs do double duty: they carry meaning and they create a mental image. "Launch", "cut", "double", "fix", "ship" — each one tells the reader exactly what happens, while vague verbs leave them guessing. Before you publish anything, scan for weak verbs and replace at least three of them. Your reader will feel the difference even if they cannot name it.

**Why it matters:** Readers trust writers who sound certain — precise verbs make your copy sound like it was written by someone who knows exactly what they are talking about.
