# Retouch Non-Destructively: Your Original Pixels Should Never Be at Risk

Every retouch you will ever regret starts the same way: editing directly on the original layer. Once pixels are overwritten, there is no undo after the file is saved — and clients always ask for "the version before that last change."

Work non-destructively instead. First, convert your background layer into a Smart Object (or duplicate it and hide the original) so filters stay editable. Do all tone and color work on adjustment layers — Curves, Hue/Saturation, Color Balance — and paint on their masks instead of erasing pixels. Clone, heal, and dodge-and-burn on separate blank layers set to the right blend mode, so each fix can be dialed back or deleted on its own. Finish with a stamped "before" snapshot (a flattened duplicate at the bottom of the stack) so you can always A/B your retouch against the original.

This discipline costs you nothing in speed once it becomes habit, and it turns every revision request from a rebuild into a slider tweak.

**Why it matters:** Non-destructive editing protects your original image, makes client revisions painless, and keeps every technique you used visible and reversible — the mark of a professional retoucher.
