# Frequency Separation: Clean Skin Without the Plastic Look

Frequency separation splits a portrait into two layers: low frequency (color and tone) and high frequency (texture and fine detail). You retouch each independently — smooth out blotchy color on the low layer with a soft brush, and remove blemishes on the high layer with the healing brush — so the skin keeps its natural pores. Done right, the face looks polished but real; done wrong, you get the plastic doll look that screams "over-retouched." Build the separation on duplicate layers, never on the original pixels, so every edit stays fully reversible. And check your work at 50–100% zoom — retouching at 400% zoom is how you end up with unnatural results you only notice after exporting.

**Why it matters:** Clients can spot over-retouched skin instantly, and nothing says "amateur" faster than a face with zero texture. Frequency separation gives you control instead of compromise.
