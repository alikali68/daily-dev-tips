# Remove Color Casts Before You Retouch Anything

A photo with a warm tungsten cast or a green fluorescent tint will fight every edit you make after it. Skin tones go muddy, whites never look white, and your color grading sits on top of a lie. Before any retouching, neutralize the cast: sample a neutral gray or white area with the white-balance eyedropper, or adjust temperature and tint until a known neutral reads neutral. Work on a duplicate layer or an adjustment layer so the correction stays non-destructive. Only once the neutrals are truly neutral should you move on to exposure, contrast, and skin work — everything after it gets easier and more predictable.

**Why it matters:** A clean white balance is the foundation every later adjustment builds on; fix it first and your retouching takes half the effort and looks twice as natural.
