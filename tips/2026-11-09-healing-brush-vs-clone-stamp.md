# Healing Brush vs. Clone Stamp: Use the Right Tool for the Right Fix

The Clone Stamp copies pixels exactly as they are. The Healing Brush copies texture too, but blends the color and light with the area around it — so the fix disappears instead of leaving a visible patch.

For skin retouching, reach for the Healing Brush first. It borrows the surrounding tone, so blemishes, dust spots, and small distractions vanish while the skin's natural texture stays intact. Reserve the Clone Stamp for hard edges and defined shapes: removing a stray hair against a background, cleaning up a product edge, or duplicating a pattern where an exact copy is what you need.

Work on a separate empty layer, sample from a clean area close to your target, and use a soft brush at low opacity with several light passes instead of one heavy stroke. Always zoom to 100% before you finish — a fix that looks perfect at 25% often shows a smudged or repeated texture at full size.

**Why it matters:** choosing the tool that matches the job is what separates invisible retouching from obvious edits — clients notice clean work, not the hours behind it.
