# Cut the First Paragraph — Your Real Opening Is Hiding Under It

Most first paragraphs are throat-clearing: background, apologies, and slow warm-ups the writer needed but the reader doesn't. Write the whole piece, then delete the first paragraph and read what remains — in most cases, the real opening was hiding in paragraph two all along.

This works because writers think onto the page: the first paragraph is where you figure out what you actually want to say. Readers, on the other hand, decide in seconds whether to keep going, and they judge you by the first lines they see — not by the warm-up you needed to get there.

Try it on your next post, README, or landing page: cut the opener, then read the new first line out loud. If it still makes sense, you just made your writing sharper. If something is genuinely missing, add back only the one sentence that carries it — not the whole paragraph.

**Why it matters:** The first lines decide whether anyone reads the rest — starting where the value starts respects the reader's time and keeps them scrolling.
